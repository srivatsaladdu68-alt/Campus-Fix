From Breakdown to Resolution in 6 Clear Stages

Campus Fix brings complete accountability to university facilities management. Every complaint is tracked from the moment it is reported until work is confirmed and closed.

Complaint Lifecycle

The 6-Stage Resolution Pipeline

Every request progresses through a standardized state machine with full audit logging.

3

1

Step 1: Fault Reported by Student or Faculty

The user selects the facility category (Electrical, Washroom, Classroom Equipment, IT, etc.), campus building, floor, and room number. They add problem symptoms and optionally attach a photo. An automated unique tracking code (e.g. CF-2026-1495) is generated, and a deadline is computed according to priority.

2

Step 2: Administrative Verification

Campus facilities administrators review new incoming reports. They verify legitimacy, eliminate duplicates or spam, adjust priority if safety hazards exist, and confirm facility accessibility.

3

Step 3: Specialized Technician Assignment

The administrator dispatches the work ticket to the qualified staff member (e.g., Electrician for power faults, Plumber for pipe leaks). The technician receives an immediate in-app notification with location details and targeted deadline.

4

Step 4: Repair Execution & "In Progress" Updates

The technician accepts the work order and begins on-site inspection. The status switches to In Progress. The technician logs repair notes and can check a box to flag administrators if extra replacement parts or vendor assistance is required.

5

Step 5: Completion & "Resolved" Notification

Upon replacing broken parts and testing the equipment, the technician records completion notes and marks the ticket as Resolved. The student or faculty reporter immediately receives an alert confirming the fix.

6

Step 6: Final Closure or User Reopen

The complaint can be officially marked Closed. However, if the issue recurs within a few hours or days (e.g. the AC trips again), the reporter can simply click Reopen Complaint, returning it to the queue with high-priority dispatch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions & System Rules

How does duplicate detection help technicians?

If ten students in the same lecture hall report a broken projector simultaneously, the duplicate detection engine immediately alerts each student that the issue has already been logged, preventing 10 duplicate work orders from cluttering the technician queue.

Who can create staff or administrator accounts?

In accordance with security requirements, staff and admin roles cannot be self-selected on the public registration form. They must be registered directly by an authorized campus facilities administrator.

Can I track a complaint without logging in?

Students and staff can navigate to the Track Complaints page to search for complaints by code or room number, while full editing and reopen privileges remain protected by authentication.
